01

Potential Problems in the Current System

14 high-risk vulnerabilities and operational bottlenecks identified in current manual operations

Before writing code or architecting solutions, we must catalog the concrete failure modes that plague day-to-day cash disbursements and reconciliations:

1
Expenses are recorded manually without standardized entry gates.
2
Receipts and paper vouchers can get easily misplaced or lost.
3
Management cannot immediately see how much cash has been issued in real time.
4
Employees frequently forget line items they spent money on during trips.
5
Lack of consistent, company-wide expense categories and taxonomy.
6
Extremely difficult to cross-compare money disbursed vs. money actually spent.
7
Remaining balances and cash surpluses are not properly tracked or surrendered.
8
Disjointed approvals happen via WhatsApp, paper scraps, informal chats, and Excel.
9
Complete absence of an immutable, tamper-evident audit trail.
10
Management cannot definitively determine who authorized a specific transaction.
11
Accounting spends days manually consolidating fragmented spreadsheets and receipts.
12
Historical expense trends and past transaction records are difficult or impossible to retrieve.
13
Fraudulent submissions, inflated costs, or accidental misuse are difficult to detect.
14
Management reporting requires exhausting manual effort and produces delayed figures.
02

Define the PURPOSE

The foundational reason why this software platform must exist

System Purpose Statement
“To centralize, control, track, and report company expenses from the moment funds are requested or issued through spending, receipt submission, review, approval, reconciliation, and reporting.”

This single statement anchors every functional decision. Any proposed feature that does not directly advance this lifecycle boundary must be scrutinized or excluded.

03

Define the OBJECTIVES

Translating corporate purpose into measurable, concrete engineering outcomes

👁️
Objective 1: Expense Visibility

Management must possess 360-degree real-time visibility into:

  • Who received company funds
  • How much was issued & disbursement method
  • Why the money was issued (business purpose)
  • What line items it was spent on
  • How much was actually spent vs remaining
  • Official approval status and authorization logs
👤
Objective 2: Accountability

Every single shilling issued must have an unambiguous, identifiable person responsible for it until formal sign-off.

Concrete Example:
Paul → Travel Advance → UGX 500,000 → Kampala–Mbarara trip → Expenses → UGX 420,000 → Balance UGX 80,000
🧾
Objective 3: Expense Documentation

The system must mandate and associate digital supporting documentation (receipts, fuel vouchers, invoices, photos) with individual expense line items before closure.

🛡️
Objective 4: Approval Control

Enforce strict corporate authorization hierarchies. Unapproved funds cannot be disbursed; unverified expenses cannot be written off as reconciled.

⚖️
Objective 5: Reconciliation

Automate balance determination to guarantee zero unallocated cash floats:

Rule Money Issued − Legitimate Expenses = Balance

Configured according to the company's official financial chart of accounts.

📊
Objective 6: Instant Reporting

Allow management and accounting to produce comprehensive expenditure analytics, balance sheets, and tax reports without manually consolidating disparate spreadsheets.

🔍
Objective 7: Auditability & Non-Repudiation

When money is involved, every ledger entry must answer four immutable questions:

Who created this record?
Identity & authenticated user session
Who modified or adjusted it?
Change logs, diffs, and justifications
Who authorized/approved it?
Sign-off authority at each gate
When did they do it?
Accurate, server-validated timestamps

Stakeholder Identification & Role Mapping

Identifying everyone who touches company money and why they care

Never assume “the users are just employees.” Different roles interact with financial data in completely distinct ways.

Stakeholder Role Core Concerns & Operational Motivations Key System Interactions
CEO / Managing Director Total financial visibility, control over company runway, fraud prevention, high-value approvals Executive dashboards, advance approvals, macro reports
Operations Manager Operational budgets, project/travel cost adherence, team schedule enablement Reviews advance requests, validates task justification
Accountant / Finance Team Accuracy, tax compliance, receipts verification, balance reconciliation, general ledger exports Disburses funds, verifies receipts, reconciles balances
Staff Employee Fast disbursement of funds, simple receipt uploads via mobile, clear status of balances Submits advance requests, uploads receipts, returns balances
Driver / Field Agent Quick recording of fuel, parking, tolls, vehicle maintenance on mobile devices with low data usage Quick photo uploads, fuel and per diem tracking
Designated Approver Clarity on purpose of request, rapid 1-click approvals without paper bottlenecks Reviews notifications, approves or rejects with reasons
System Administrator User provisioning, permission security, role-based access control (RBAC), data backups Manages categories, audit logs, system configuration
Internal / External Auditor Independent verification of historical expenditures, tax compliance, tamper checking Read-only export access to historical ledger and attachments
⚠️

Rule of Grounded Discovery: Do Not Invent Roles!

Always interview the company to discover their true, on-the-ground operational structure. For example, maybe there is no Operations Manager and the CEO approves everything directly; maybe there are two accountants; or maybe drivers do not request cash themselves. Planning must flow directly into Requirements Analysis.

1
Phase A: Fund Issuance

Define WHAT COUNTS AS AN EXPENSE

Categorization rules, eligible expense types, and supporting documentation policies

An expense is defined as any company-related spending that requires documentation, reconciliation, and/or management authorization.

Core Expense Categories

  • ✓ Fuel (Petrol / Diesel)
  • ✓ Accommodation / Lodging
  • ✓ Meals & Subsistence
  • ✓ Public Transport & Boda-Boda
  • ✓ Parking Fees
  • ✓ Highway Tolls
  • ✓ Emergency Vehicle Maintenance
  • ✓ Communication & Internet Airtime
  • ✓ Direct Office / Field Supplies
  • ✓ Client Entertainment / Refreshments
  • ✓ Fixed Per Diem Travel Allowances
  • ✓ Approved Miscellaneous Expenses
💡

Category-Specific Validation Rules (Requirements Transition)

Different categories must enforce distinct business rules in the software:

  • Fuel: Mandatory photo of pump receipt + current vehicle odometer reading.
  • Accommodation: Official hotel tax invoice showing guest name and dates.
  • Per Diem Allowances: Fixed company rate without requiring micro-receipts.

Money Flows & Policy Baseline

Formal operational rules governing requests, custody, disbursements, and deficits

Because the platform governs real company capital, the money flow rules must be explicitly codified:

Where does money originate?
Dedicated Company Petty Cash Reserve
Who initiates the request?
The Employee / Assigned Team Lead
Who authorizes / approves?
Managing Director (MD) / Designated Approver
Who physically releases the funds?
Managing Director (MD) / Custodian
How is cash disbursed?
Physical Cash (Option for Mobile Money / Bank)
How is spending recorded?
Individual Line Entries initially, then Batch Submissions
How are receipts documented?
Physical Paper Archive & In-App Photograph
When Spending < Advance?
Employee returns remaining balance directly to Cash Custodian
When Spending > Advance?
Employee files Supplemental Reimbursement Claim with receipts
When an expense is rejected?
Employee refunds the company, or Management handles it via payroll offset

Define the PROBLEM BOUNDARY

Strictly defining In-Scope vs Out-of-Scope features to prevent scope explosion

A major failure pattern in internal software is scope creep (e.g. “Since we are tracking expenses, let's also build a full payroll and inventory system!”). Defining hard boundaries protects the project budget and timeline.

✓ Potentially IN Scope
  • Cash Advance Requests & Approvals
  • Advance Issuance & Custody Logging
  • Line-Item Expense Recording
  • Digital Receipt Camera Uploads & Viewing
  • Multi-tier Approval / Rejection Workflows
  • Advance Reconciliation & Surplus Balancing
  • Standard Expense Categories & Policies
  • User Roles, Authentication & Permissions
  • Management Dashboards & Expense Reports
  • Tamper-Evident Audit History & Timestamps
  • Email & System Notifications
✕ Strictly OUT of Scope (Phase 1)
  • Full Corporate Accounting / General Ledger
  • Employee Payroll & Salary Processing
  • Direct Automated Core Banking Integrations
  • National Tax Filing / Electronic Fiscal Systems
  • Warehouse Inventory & Stock Management
  • Vendor Procurement & Purchase Orders
  • Human Resource Management (HRIS)
  • GPS Vehicle Fleet Telematics
🛑

Protecting the Project Scope

When stakeholders suggest adding unrelated systems six months later, remind them: That is a separate scope discussion with its own charter, budget, and timeline.

Establish PROJECT CONSTRAINTS

Technical, budgetary, environmental, and human realities governing development

💵
Financial Budget
How much capital can the company allocate for engineering, infrastructure, licensing, and ongoing maintenance?
👥
People & Team Capacity
Development is driven by the lead engineer and technical director. Are there additional developers, or must architecture stay lightweight and maintainable?
⏱️
Timeline to MVP
When does the company demand the first functional, usable release to replace vulnerable paper procedures?
☁️
Hosting Infrastructure
Cloud VM vs. Company Server vs. Dedicated VPS (IONOS / DigitalOcean). Needs secure SSL, PostgreSQL database engine, and automated nightly backups.
📶
Network Connectivity
Field staff and drivers will log expenses in upcountry areas where mobile 3G/4G connectivity is intermittent. Uploads must be compressed and resilient.
📱
Target Devices
Management & Finance operate on Desktop / Laptop (Chrome, Edge). Field staff operate on Android smartphones and iPhones. Responsive mobile web is essential.
🔒
Security & Privacy
Financial data requires encrypted connections (HTTPS), role-segregated databases, protected receipt storage, and hardened authentication.
🔄
Existing Tools & Legacy Flow
Excel sheets, WhatsApp receipts, and paper vouchers currently dominate. Migration strategies must assist users rather than create sudden friction.

Four-Dimensional Feasibility Study

Determining realistically whether this system can and should be built

💻
1. Technical Feasibility

Proposed Stack: Laravel + React + PostgreSQL

While technically sound, we must answer:

  • Does our team have the production proficiency?
  • Can we securely maintain and update it over years?
  • Can we handle image compression and reliable cloud storage?
  • Can we guarantee automated automated database backups?
  • Can we optimize for low-bandwidth mobile devices?
⚙️
2. Operational Feasibility

“You can build the most beautiful expense system in Uganda. If the accountant says ‘I prefer my Excel sheet,’ you have failed.”

  • Will employees find receipt uploads simple enough?
  • Will busy managers actually approve in the system?
  • Does it align with actual company disbursement steps?
  • What structured user training is necessary for rollout?
📈
3. Economic Feasibility

Cost vs. Value Equation:

Dev + Hosting + Maintenance < Time Saved + Leakage Prevented

Internal software earns ROI by eliminating financial leakage, recovering surplus cash floats, and reclaiming hundreds of administrative hours.

⚖️
4. Legal & Compliance

Financial governance requires considering:

  • Data privacy and financial confidentiality
  • Strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
  • Tax document retention standards
  • Non-repudiation of approvals and transactions
  • Off-site automated encrypted backups

Define SUCCESS

How we quantitatively verify that the platform solved the real business problem

⚠️

Do Not Confuse "Software Works" with "Project Success"

Never say: “The project is successful when the software works without bugs.” Software can be defect-free while failing completely to solve the company's financial chaos.

Key Success Metrics (KPIs)

  • ✓ 100% of travel advances recorded digitally prior to disbursement.
  • ✓ Every spent shilling linked to an accountable, authenticated employee.
  • ✓ Supporting receipts attached and instantly retrievable on 95%+ of claims.
  • ✓ Real-time executive dashboard displaying total outstanding cash advances.
  • ✓ Finance reconciles travel expenses without opening a single manual spreadsheet.
  • ✓ Full traceability for all approved, rejected, and amended vouchers.
  • ✓ 1-click monthly expenditure summary reports delivered on demand.
  • ✓ Staff submit and reconcile claims with minimal training or friction.
  • ✓ Instant retrieval of historical financial records during internal/external audits.

Current Process Discovery: “Follow the Money”

The single most powerful requirements discovery technique for financial software

🎙️

The Golden Interview Rule

Never ask stakeholders: “What features do you want in the app?” That invites fantasy feature-lists. Instead, say:
“Show me the last real travel expense from beginning to end.”

Real-World Case Study: “Yesterday John needed UGX 500,000 to travel to Mbarara.” We follow the chain:

01
Why did he need the money?
Operational client meeting, field site visit, and vehicle fuel in Mbarara.
02
Who requested it?
John (Field Officer) via written note / verbal request to supervisor.
03
Who approved it?
Managing Director authorized the amount before any disbursement occurred.
04
Who gave him the money?
MD / Petty Cash Custodian opened safe and counted the currency.
05
How was it received?
Physical cash banknotes handed over at the head office.
06
What did he actually spend?
UGX 220,000 fuel + UGX 100,000 hotel + UGX 60,000 meals + UGX 40,000 tolls = UGX 420,000.
07
Where are the receipts?
Fuel station paper printout, hotel invoice, toll tickets stuffed into wallet.
08
Who checked them upon return?
Accountant inspected papers, cross-checking totals against vouchers.
09
What happened to the remaining UGX 80,000?
Returned in cash to safe and recorded as balance surrendered.
10
Where was everything recorded?
Handwritten petty cash logbook and an offline Excel spreadsheet.
11
What report does management ultimately demand?
Monthly project cost report showing travel expenditure vs budget.

That single walkthrough uncovers more real requirements than weeks of developers brainstorming abstract features.

The Brutal Truth: The Deliverables Gate

Why calendar schedules do not dictate when planning is complete

⚡

The Calendar Fallacy

Saying: “We have allocated two solid days for planning, therefore planning must finish today” is dangerous. The calendar does not determine whether planning is complete — the deliverables and answers do.

If you cannot definitively answer these 7 fundamental questions, planning is not finished, regardless of the clock:

Q1
What business problem are we solving?
Clear understanding of friction, delays, and cash loss.
Q2
Who has the problem?
Field employees, accounting staff, and executive leadership.
Q3
How does the current process work?
Grounded reality from real recent travel examples.
Q4
Why is software necessary?
Why existing spreadsheets or paper forms fail at scale.
Q5
What does success look like?
Quantifiable business KPIs, not just code execution.
Q6
What is in vs out of scope?
Airtight boundaries preventing scope explosion.
Q7
Is the project technically, operationally, economically, and legally feasible?
Spending an extra 4 hours discovering that the accountant urgently requires advance reconciliation, while you were about to build a generic receipt uploader, will save weeks of wasted programming.

The SDLC Separation: Transition to Requirements

Maintaining clean architectural discipline across software lifecycle stages

Stage 1

Planning asks:

“Should we build this, why are we building it, and what are the boundaries?”

Stage 2

Requirements asks:

“Exactly what must the system do, for whom, under what rules and conditions?”

Stage 3

Design asks:

“How are we going to engineer and architect a system that satisfies those requirements?”

⚠️

Engineering Discipline Warning

Do not design database schemas yet. Do not choose tables yet. Do not draw UI mockups yet.

1. First establish the Business Reality.
2. Turn that reality into verified Requirements.
3. Turn those requirements into Architecture & Design.
4. Then — and only then — start writing code.
